In 2007 I was stitching couches in Irbid. By day, foam, fabric and a staple gun. By night, PlayStation. If you'd told me then that I'd spend my working life arguing about border radius, I would have asked what a border radius was.

## The couch years

Upholstery taught me the first rule of design before I knew it was a rule: people don't care how it's made, they care whether they can sit on it comfortably. A seam that looks perfect and pinches someone's leg is a bad seam. I still think about screens that way.

It also taught me patience with things that don't fit. Fabric never lines up the first time. Neither does a navigation menu.

## The degree

In 2010 I graduated in graphic design from Irbid National University, with honours. My mom was proud. My dad asked when I'd get a real job. Both reactions were reasonable.

## Drawing for a living

Then came Square Design and more than 300 illustrated stories for children's books. It was the first time someone paid me to draw, and it turned into the best UX training I ever had, though nobody called it that. ([I wrote about that here](/blog/300-childrens-books).)

## The news years

In 2018 I moved into motion and visual design for Al Jazeera, building visuals for social platforms that reached millions of people, then TRT Arabi in 2019, designing motion and user experiences for Arabic digital platforms. News is a strange school. You learn to design while everything is breaking, and to make something clear in the time it takes a headline to change.

## Products

In 2021 I became lead UI/UX designer at Syntax, responsible for the digital products, the interaction models, the user flows and the specs. In 2025 I became Director of UX at Sitech and started my own consultancy on the side, working with government, fintech, edtech and game companies across the region.

Somewhere in between I started teaching, co-hosting a podcast, and drawing UX explainers on Instagram as @alaa_aliens. And recently I started building apps for my two daughters, who are the hardest clients I've ever had.

## What stayed the same

Every job on this list was the same job wearing different clothes: take something complicated and make it comfortable for the person using it. Couches, children's books, breaking news, dashboards. Same seam, different fabric.

> I started out stitching couches. Now I stitch pixels. Both jobs are about making something people can sit with comfortably.
