Ftoor Designer (فطور ديزاينر, "designer's breakfast") is the podcast I co-hosted with Osama Khalil. The pitch was simple: for designers only, your creative dose with your morning coffee. Two designers talking about the actual job, in Arabic, the way we'd talk about it over breakfast.

It ran from 2021 to 2025, 57 episodes, and made the top ten design podcasts in the region. You can still find it on [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/podcast/id1549380031), Spotify and Anghami.

## Why breakfast

Every episode is named after something you might eat for breakfast. Kiwi. Cheesecake. Qatayef. Spinach pies. Iced water. An empty plate. It started as a joke and became the show's personality. You'd see the menu before you saw the topic, and the food usually had something to do with it if you squinted.

## What we talked about

Not tools, mostly. Tools change every six months. We talked about the parts of the job nobody teaches:

- **The designer is a businessperson** (Kiwi). How handling clients badly follows you into your next project.
- **Passion or money?** (Iced water). Chasing a bigger salary versus doing work you love for less.
- **Don't be clever in design, be a genius** (Spinach pies). Why simple usually wins.
- **Designers and developers** (Omelette, and Qatayef). A long history of friction, and how to work together anyway.
- **Product managers and designers** (Cheesecake), with Kareem El Masri. Is the love real?
- **Listen before you work with a designer** (Empty plate). An episode for clients, about their side of the work.
- **The obsession with job titles** (Oats). Who decides that you're "senior"?
- **Mental health for designers** (Morning coffee). Looking after yourself in an office and as a freelancer.

We had guests who changed careers late, guests from product and development, and even one episode about fast fashion that had nothing to do with screens.

## The break and the comeback

We took a break of almost a year, then came back at the end of 2024 with an episode about walking into 2025 as designers, answering listener questions about tools, trends and favourite designs. The last episodes asked bigger questions: whether social media has become designers' main source of inspiration, how design companies drain designers' wallets, and whether Arab Bank's new branding would survive the controversy.

## What it gave me

A podcast forces you to have an opinion out loud, then defend it next to a co-host who's allowed to push back. That's good practice for every design review I've had since.

And it was a way to talk to designers across the region in our own language, about the parts of the job that usually get discussed only in English, or not at all.
