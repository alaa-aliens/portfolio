When you talk about design every week for four years, a few ideas keep coming back no matter what the episode was supposed to be about. These are the ones that stuck with me from [Ftoor Designer](/blog/ftoor-designer).

## 1. Clients are half of the work

We did a whole episode on the idea that the designer is a businessperson, and another one aimed at clients: listen before you work with a designer. Put them together and you get something I believe more every year. The design is only as good as the conversation around it. A great screen in a badly handled project doesn't ship, and the bad handling follows you into the next project.

## 2. Simple is harder than clever

"Don't be clever in design, be a genius" was one of my favourite titles. Clever is the extra animation, the hidden gesture, the metaphor you need to explain. Genius is when nobody notices the design because the thing just works. It takes more drafts to get to simple, not fewer.

## 3. Titles are noise, skills are signal

We spent an episode on the obsession with job titles, and another asking what experience really means beyond certificates and years. The answer we kept landing on: your experience is measured by the problems you've survived, not the date on your CV. A junior who has shipped something hard knows more than a senior who has only shipped easy things.

## 4. Developers are not the enemy

Designers and developers came up again and again: an omelette episode about the history of rivalry, a qatayef episode about friction with app developers. The fix was always the same and always boring. Talk earlier. Show the messy version. Ask what's expensive to build before you fall in love with it.

## 5. Look after yourself

One of the shortest episodes was about mental health for designers, and it might be the most important. The job is endless feedback on things you care about. If you don't build a way to separate your work from your worth, the feedback will do it for you, badly.

## The one I still argue about

Passion or money. We did a whole episode on it (Iced water) and I still don't have a clean answer. Sometimes the work you love pays badly, and sometimes a good salary is what buys you time for the work you love. I suspect most designers live somewhere in between, and that's fine.
