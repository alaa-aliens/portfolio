I've worked with a lot of clients: newsrooms like Al Jazeera and TRT, game companies like Riot Games, Jawaker and Tamatem, startups through Endeavor Jordan and Zain, companies like Carseer and BravoBravo, and government teams. The products were all different. The projects that went well had the same beginning.

## Can you say the brief in one line?

The best brief I ever got was one line: minimal, easy access, group the data, no new features. If a client can't say what success looks like in a sentence, we're not ready to design. We're ready to talk.

## Who decides?

Every project has someone whose "yes" actually counts. I want to know who it is on day one, not after the fourth round of feedback from someone who turns out not to be them.

## Can we agree structure before colour?

I ask to show grey boxes first, with notes. Clients sometimes find it slow. It's the fastest route I know, because it stops the "I don't like the blue" meetings before they start. ([More on that here](/blog/grey-boxes-first).)

## Can we write the reasons down?

Next to the designs I keep a board of what we borrowed, adapted or skipped from other products, and why. On longer projects I also keep a short state document, so every new round picks up exactly where the last one stopped. Nobody has to remember why a decision was made. It's written next to it.

## Who is actually using this?

Not "users". Which person, doing what, where? A dispatcher watching a hundred trucks. A nervous car buyer in a car park. An eight-year-old who can't read every word yet. Once we can picture one real person, most design arguments settle themselves.

## What I promise back

Rounds, not reveals. You'll see the messy version early and often, and you're allowed to hate it. That's what it's for.
