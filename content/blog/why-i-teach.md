I've been teaching design for a long time now, and in a lot of different rooms. Design-thinking sessions as a guest lecturer at HTU. UX/UI training for internal teams at Zain. Cohorts at Takween. A yearly product design program for kids with YES JO and Zain, where children learn Figma. Workshops for aspiring designers at 42 Amman. UX/UI courses across Jordan's government universities with Zain. And now with the IEEE community.

People ask why I keep doing it on top of a full-time job. Honest answer: it's selfish.

## Teaching is a test I take

If I can't explain something to a room of beginners, with a marker and a whiteboard, I don't understand it yet. I just know the vocabulary. Teaching is where I find out which one it is.

That's also why I draw almost everything I teach. A drawing doesn't let you hide behind jargon. Either the boxes and arrows make sense or they don't.

## The best questions come from the back row

Experienced designers ask about tools. Beginners ask "why?" And "why?" is the question that breaks lazy habits. Why is this button here? Why does this screen need a manual? Why do we call it onboarding when people just want to start? I've changed how I work because of questions asked by people in their first week.

## Kids are the harshest room

The kids' program is my favourite. Children don't pretend to be interested. If the exercise is boring, they tell you, immediately, with their whole body. Some of them end up faster in Figma than adults I know.

## Choosing who's in the room

For one 42 Amman workshop we had 134 applicants and room for 70, so I built a scoring system to choose fairly. It was more work than the workshop itself, and worth it. A room of people who really want to be there changes everything.

## What I want people to leave with

Not shortcuts. Shortcuts change every year. I want them to leave with one habit: look at a screen and ask what the person using it is trying to do, and whether the screen is helping or getting in the way.

Sometimes they clap. Sometimes they just want the Figma shortcuts. Both count.
