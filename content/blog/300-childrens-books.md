Before dashboards and design systems, I spent years drawing children's books at Square Design. More than 300 illustrated stories. My hand still remembers every one.

I didn't know it then, but it was the best UX training I ever had.

## The reader can't ask you

A seven-year-old turning a page has no one to ask "what's happening here?". The picture has to answer before the question forms. That's exactly the situation of a person opening your app for the first time. You won't be sitting next to them.

## One idea per page

The pages that worked had one thing happening. The ones that failed tried to tell three moments at once. Screens are the same: one main action, everything else quieter.

## Keep the character the same

If the hero's scarf changes colour between pages, kids notice instantly and lose the thread. Users notice inconsistent buttons, labels and icons the same way, even when they can't say why something feels off.

## Pictures before words

Kids read the picture first and the text second. Most people scan an interface the same way: shape, colour and position long before they read the label.

---

I still draw most of my ideas before I open Figma. A rough sketch tells me in a minute whether a screen has one idea or three.
