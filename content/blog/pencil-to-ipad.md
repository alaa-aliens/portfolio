I've drawn on a lot of things. Here's the short version, in the order my hand learned them.

## 2007: pencil and paper

Where it all started: sketching by hand, on whatever paper was around. No undo button, which is the best drawing teacher there is. You learn to commit to a line.

## 2012: Wacom and Illustrator

Drawing professionally meant going digital. A Wacom tablet and Adobe Illustrator, which is where the 300+ children's book stories at Square Design came from. Looking at your hand on the desk while the line appears on a screen takes months to get used to.

## 2015: the first iPad

My first iPad for drawing on the go. It wasn't a serious drawing tool yet, but it meant the sketchbook came with me everywhere.

## 2018: a better iPad

Better screen, better stylus, better doodles. Each step made the gap between thinking something and drawing it a little smaller.

## 2020: Apple Pencil and Procreate

This one changed everything. An iPad Pro, an Apple Pencil and Procreate made drawing on a screen feel like drawing on paper again, but with layers and an undo button. It's still the setup I use most.

## 2022: @alaa_aliens

With a newer iPad Pro I started drawing UX explainers on Instagram as @alaa_aliens: hand-drawn posts about design, usability and the daily comedy of working with screens. Drawing them is half explaining, half therapy.

## Why I draw before I design

Tools change every few years. The habit doesn't. I still draw most ideas before I open Figma, for three reasons:

- **A sketch is honest.** It can't hide a messy idea behind nice colours and fonts.
- **A sketch is fast.** In a minute I know whether a screen has one idea or three.
- **A sketch invites people in.** Nobody is afraid to scribble on a drawing. Everyone is afraid to comment on a polished mockup.

> The tool matters less than the habit. Draw first, decide second, polish last.
