A lot of my consulting is reviewing products that already exist: product audits and UX reviews for Endeavor Jordan's portfolio companies, UX strategy and product evaluation for startups at Zain, experience evaluation for digital media at TRT World.

Someone built a thing. Something feels off. They want to know what. Here's how I do it.

## Use it like a real person first

Before any checklist, I try to do the main job the product exists for, the way a real user would, on the device they'd use. Most of the important problems show up in the first ten minutes, while I'm still confused in the same way a new user is.

## Find the main job

Every product has one job people open it for. Live tracking in a fleet app. Getting from a VIN to a report on a car history site. A review that doesn't start there spends its time on the edges.

## Pick three

An audit with a hundred findings is a document nobody opens twice. I sort everything I find and pick the three that matter most, the ones that would change how the product feels if they were fixed. The rest goes in an appendix.

## Say it kindly

The people in the room built this thing, usually under pressure, usually with good reasons for every decision. "This is broken" helps nobody. "Here's where people get stuck, here's why, here's what we could try" gets things fixed.

## Show, don't list

Wherever I can, I sketch the fix next to the problem. A rough drawing of a better screen does more than a paragraph explaining what's wrong with the current one.

## Stay for the fixing

The review isn't the product. The fix is. The best audits I've done ended with us working on the three things together, round after round, until they were actually better.

> You built a thing. Something feels off. I find it. Then we fix it together.
